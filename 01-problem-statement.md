# Problem statement

## One-liner
**More than 1 in 4 Michigan students miss nearly a month of school a year. Schools treat them as problems to fix, never as people who could lead.**

## Problem statement
In the 2025-26 school year, 27.2% of Michigan students were chronically absent, missing 18 or more days of school (MDE, Sept 2026). The problem grows in high school: about 35.7% of 12th graders are chronically absent, and students often start drifting away around 10th grade. About 38% of economically disadvantaged students are chronically absent, compared with 15% of their peers, and in Detroit the rate is 60% (Bridge Michigan, Sept 2026).

Students rarely stay home because they don't care. A late bus, falling behind, family responsibilities, or feeling like no one notices or needs them makes skipping easier than showing up. Schools respond with calls home, warnings and perfect-attendance rewards, which treat absent students as problems to manage. Nothing gives them a reason to be there that matters to *them*: someone counting on them.

## Point of view
High schoolers who are slipping need to feel **needed**, not nagged. When someone is counting on them, showing up becomes a responsibility they choose, not a rule they're breaking.

## How might we
How might we give students who are missing class a reason to show up by making them mentors that younger students count on, and rewarding them for it?

## Evidence that this works
The IDRA Valued Youth Partnership has turned at-risk students into tutors of younger students since 1984. It has kept 98% of its tutors in school, and tutors show higher attendance and lower discipline rates.

## Success metric
Higher attendance among student mentors, fewer chronically absent students, and fewer 10th-grade drop-offs.
