# Impact, scale and Michigan

## Why it matters
- 27.2% of Michigan students were chronically absent in 2025-26; about 60% in Detroit.
- Chronic absence peaks in high school (about 35.7% of 12th graders) and students start drifting around 10th grade.
- More than a quarter of Detroiters aged 16-24 are neither in school nor working.

## What we'd measure
- Mentor attendance (before vs. during MOVA)
- Mentee attendance and on-time rate
- 10th-grade drop-off and on-track-to-graduate rates
- Help requests answered within a day; mentor log completion

## How it scales
- One teacher advisor runs about 6 crews of 5 mentees (36+ students reached) in about 5 minutes a day.
- Pilot in Detroit, then Pontiac, Flint and Saginaw, then statewide.

## Business value
- Districts lose outcomes and funding to chronic absence. MOVA would be sold as a district license, funded through at-risk and Title I money (how in-person mentoring programs like Valued Youth are funded today).

## Keeping value, jobs and opportunity in Michigan
- More students graduating means a stronger Michigan workforce.
- Mentors build real leadership experience, service credit and résumé letters, plus a summer job fast-track with local employers.
- Built in Michigan, by Michigan talent, for Michigan schools.

> Detroit put the world on wheels. Now it puts its students in the driver's seat.
