# Personas

## Primary persona: the mentor
**Jaylen Brooks, 16, 10th grade, Detroit**
*"Nobody at school needs me. If I don't show up, nobody notices."*

- Takes two DDOT buses to school. Misses about 1–2 days a week, mostly Mondays and first period. On track to be chronically absent.
- Fell behind in math after missing days, so class feels pointless when he does go.
- Funny, well liked, the "big brother" type at home: he looks after his younger cousins.

**Goals:** feel respected and useful; earn something real (credit, rewards, recognition); graduate.
**Frustrations:** adults only reach out to scold him; one missed day knocks him out of perfect-attendance rewards; a late bus makes being late feel like a reason to skip.
**Needs from MOVA:** a real role with 5 younger students who count on him; a simple way to check on them; credit and rewards that grow when he and his mentees show up; forgiving streaks; backup when things go wrong.

## Secondary persona: the mentee
**Kayla Nguyen, 14, 9th grade, Detroit**
*"High school is huge. I don't know anyone, and I'm scared to ask."*

- New to a big high school. Shy, anxious about getting lost and falling behind. Has already missed 3 days this fall.
**Needs from MOVA:** an older student who gets it; gentle morning check-ins; points and badges for showing up as part of a crew.

## Supporting persona: the teacher advisor
**Mr. Robinson, 52, math teacher and MOVA program advisor**
*"If it takes more than five minutes a week, I can't do it."*

- Oversees about 6 mentor crews (around 36 students). Not technical.
**Needs from MOVA:** one simple screen showing who needs help; one-tap approval of mentor hours and credit; alerts only for serious issues; a FERPA-safe setup through the school.

## Why Jaylen is the hero
Most attendance tools treat students like Jaylen as the problem. MOVA makes him the solution.
